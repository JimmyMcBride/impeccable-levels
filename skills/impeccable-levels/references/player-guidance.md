# Player guidance without removing curiosity

**Source-derived capsule:** Shaver records misleading jump/grab affordances and retests repairs (p. 37), varies landmark visibility (p. 93), qualifies funnels by mobility (p. 136), and cautions against early breadcrumbs (p. 154). His blockmesh advice assumes established mechanics and metrics (p. 199). These are one-based PDF pages. [S8](sources-design.md#s8--invisible-intuition).

## Proposed operational guidance

Everything below is Impeccable Levels' original procedure. Follow [evaluation](evaluation.md) for evidence labels. These instructions describe tests to perform, not completed runtime testing.

### 1. Define intention and permissible uncertainty

Freeze scene revision, gate state, objective, movement abilities, controller dimensions, camera/FOV and relevant accessibility settings. Separate required progression from optional invitations. Declare what may remain uncertain and how players can recover. A knowingly chosen detour can succeed; shortest-path compliance is not the goal.

Ask afterward what destination the player intended to reach and why they chose their route. Distinguish curiosity, mistaken expectations and inability to recover. Do not infer any of these from a backtrack alone.

### 2. Keep an affordance mismatch ledger

Record:

`location/state | attempted action | available action | cue hypothesis | evidence | repair | retest`

For example, investigate an attempted climb on a scenic buttress resembling approved climbable ledges. Check alternatives: controller defect, missed input, unfamiliar controls or an intentional boundary. A screenshot supports a cue hypothesis, not observed confusion.

If the geometry sends the wrong signal, test one local change: break the climbable silhouette, change the boundary profile, or clarify the actual opening. Preserve protected dimensions and the project's existing visual language. Do not add a climbing mechanic to reconcile the mismatch. Revisit the same state and record whether mistaken attempts, correct-route recognition and recovery changed; retain contradictory observations.

### 3. Test reveals beyond the preferred approach

Inspect the intended approach, a plausible off-axis approach, return traversal, and any elevated viewpoint already reachable with the player's abilities. Mark impossible cases not applicable. Record where a landmark becomes visible, where its identity becomes recognizable, and whether the next traversable connection is understandable; these can occur separately.

Use the actual camera and movement speed. A grounded funnel may be bypassed by permitted jumping or other mobility. Do not assume angled walls control gaze. Try shifting an occluder, opening a partial view, or adjusting landmark massing before constricting movement. Preserve camera clearance, optional routes and player-controlled looking.

### 4. Compare a small repair

Hold conditions constant for before/after captures and traversal; use fresh players where feasible to reduce route-learning effects. Compare the suspected structural repair with optional breadcrumb guidance only when that distinction answers the current question. Keep necessary accessibility supports active: a neutral-lighting diagnostic is not permission to remove essential guidance.

Record unresolved readability separately from deliberate mystery. Extra pickups, contrast or arrows may help, but do not describe them as repairing disconnected routes, misleading boundaries or camera collisions. Stop at the agreed revision budget and identify the next untested condition.
