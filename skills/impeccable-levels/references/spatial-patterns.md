# Spatial patterns: choose, combine, or reject

These are original Impeccable Levels design recipes. A pattern is a candidate solution to a specific problem, not a quality requirement. Select a small number that serve the brief; do not assemble a checklist level. Dimensions come from the project and subsequent tests.

## Pattern cards

### Hub with distinct destinations

- **Use when:** players repeatedly return for services, social activity or decisions.
- **Build:** place destinations around a recognizable center; make each approach distinguishable in massing or function. Keep frequent errands direct.
- **Verify:** can players find the center from each destination and choose a route without consulting an overhead map?
- **Tradeoff:** excessive spokes can become repetitive errands. A large central plaza can add walking without adding choice.

### Branch and rejoin

- **Use when:** players should exercise agency without multiplying the whole level.
- **Build:** split around an obstacle or activity, provide different information or risk, and reconnect at a recognizable location.
- **Verify:** both paths work independently; players can describe a meaningful difference; the rejoin is understandable.
- **Tradeoff:** hidden mandatory content on one branch makes the other a trap. Identical corridors create cosmetic choice.

### Loop with a returning shortcut

- **Use when:** returning through a newly understood space is valuable.
- **Build:** establish the initial route, then open a shorter connection from the progression-accessible side. Make its far-side destination recognizable.
- **Verify:** the initial journey does not depend on the locked connection; activation is reachable; the return actually changes; both door states are tested.
- **Tradeoff:** a shortcut that saves no meaningful effort is busywork. A one-way drop can strand the player if progression state is ignored.

### Glimpse, conceal, reveal

- **Use when:** anticipation supports the experience and the destination matters.
- **Build:** offer incomplete information, allow the route to temporarily obscure it, then reveal a more understandable relationship from a usable space.
- **Verify:** record approach views in the normal camera, including lateral movement and return travel. Check what becomes known at each stage.
- **Tradeoff:** hiding everything removes anticipation; showing everything too early removes discovery. Forced camera control is a separate mechanic, not a substitute for spatial work.

### Compression and release

- **Use when:** contrasting enclosure can support relief, exposure or scale.
- **Build:** transition from a sheltered bounded space to a more open one, while preserving controller and camera clearance throughout.
- **Verify:** the contrast survives movement and the open area offers a clear next action or a deliberate pause.
- **Tradeoff:** cramped passages can become camera friction. Repeating this rhythm everywhere makes it predictable.

### Overlook, then inhabit

- **Use when:** advance knowledge makes a later route more meaningful.
- **Build:** show a future playable area from another elevation; provide a navigable transition that preserves recognizable features.
- **Verify:** players recognize the earlier view when arriving; elevation has a purpose beyond spectacle.
- **Tradeoff:** the visible destination creates an expectation of access. Do not present scenic-only space as an obvious reachable objective.

### Nested thresholds

- **Use when:** progressing through a place's layers matters: fortress, temple, industrial facility.
- **Build:** establish changes of function, access or scale between successive areas; reserve believable service circulation where relevant.
- **Verify:** players understand increasing depth without requiring a sequence of identical gates.
- **Tradeoff:** repeated chokepoints slow travel and can restrict encounter options. Architectural plausibility must serve the game, not simulate every real room.

### Recontextualized return

- **Use when:** new knowledge or a changed state can make familiar geometry interesting.
- **Build:** change what a known space means or permits, through a new viewpoint, opened connection or visible consequence.
- **Verify:** test the return with actual state changes; ask what the player now understands or can do differently.
- **Tradeoff:** merely walking backward through unchanged content is not automatically meaningful reuse.

## Adapt the pattern to the game

For an exploratory village, prioritize orientation and optional discovery. For a tactical arena, prioritize actionable relationships and encounter behavior. For a puzzle space, make changed information or state legible. For a contemplative environment, protect movement comfort and player-directed observation. A competitive map may require deliberate symmetry and predictable visibility; asymmetry is not intrinsically superior.

Use [design principles](design-principles.md) to justify the choice, [awe guidance](awe-and-perceived-scale.md) when scale is a goal, and [evaluation](evaluation.md) to test the result. These recipes are not claims that any particular published game used this implementation.
