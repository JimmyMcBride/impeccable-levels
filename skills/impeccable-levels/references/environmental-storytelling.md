# Environmental storytelling in the graybox

Use when spatial relationships should communicate a place's function, history, or consequences before detailed art.

## Source basis

Smith and Worch connect interpretation with gameplay telegraphing (PDF pp. 16–34), recommend causal chains grounded in a larger premise (42–53), warn about inconsistent player affordances (57–62), and discuss persistent player traces and revisiting spaces (63–79). See [S4](sources-design.md#s4--environmental-storytelling); page numbers are one-based. These are practitioner recommendations, not guaranteed engagement effects.

## Original Impeccable Levels procedure

Everything below is our proposed graybox translation, not the presenters' prescribed workflow.

1. **Choose a useful inference.** State what understanding could change the player's next decision or sense of the place. A working routine, communal custom, or adaptation is sufficient; destruction and combat are not prerequisites.
2. **Describe the relationship before the props.** Write `purpose or event → physical consequence → observable relationship`. Work at the game's abstraction level: access, circulation, relative placement, orientation, elevation, supports, and sightlines. Prefer a connected arrangement over scattered thematic objects.
3. **Separate required information from interpretation.** Mark what players must understand to act safely or progress, versus optional explanations of history. Support required information with readable geometry and redundant cues. Allow alternative historical interpretations when they do not mislead gameplay.
4. **Spend geometry on evidence.** Build the smallest arrangement that exposes the relationship from ordinary player views. Mark an inference unresolved when its required cues are absent or untested. Temporary text or prop cues can support a tested inference; distinguish that evidence from communication by spatial relationships alone. Do not force a geometry change when an approved placeholder already communicates the needed information.
5. **Keep agency consistent.** Do not suggest a climb, shortcut, breakable barrier, or interaction the established systems cannot support. Preserve intentional uncertainty without making false promises. Revisit visible consequences of player action only where existing mechanics support them; do not invent systems to complete the story.

## Original example: a village preparing for market

A broad service ramp connects an orchard terrace to the backs of market stalls. A pedestrian stair reaches their public fronts around a shared well. A sheltered side lane offers a quieter approach and reconnects with the courtyard. Simple stall masses, openings, and circulation suggest a community organized around shared trade without wreckage or lore panels. Walking remains the only required verb. Whether this feels welcoming is a testable hypothesis, not a property conferred by the description.

## Neutral check

Observe route choices before explaining intent. Ask: “What do you think this place is for?” “Where would you go next?” “Which routes seem usable, and why?” Record answers separately from your interpretation. Accept different backstories; repair mistaken affordances or required-route confusion. Retest the changed relationship, not just the attractiveness of its screenshot.
