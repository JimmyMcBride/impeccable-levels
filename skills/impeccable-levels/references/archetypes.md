# Choose priorities for the place and the game

These are original design lenses, not genre laws. Start with the player's verbs and the brief. Do not force every level into a dungeon with loops, a boss, and a spectacular reveal.

| Archetype | Prioritize | Useful graybox experiment | Common overcorrection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village / quest hub | Convenient repeated tasks, recognizable districts, social focus, plausible services | Enter, locate a task, take an optional detour, return without a tour guide | Winding every street to manufacture mystery; a plaza so large errands become chores |
| Exploration dungeon | Curiosity, spatial learning, distinct route consequences, useful returns | Follow the first-time route, then revisit after a gate or shortcut changes | Branches and locks with no perceptible purpose; a key behind its own gate |
| Castle / fortress | Defensible thresholds, service circulation, hierarchy, contrasts of human and monumental scale | Move the required actor/cart through gate states; inspect approach and defensive views | Treating a fantasy graybox as engineering certification; enlarging every opening equally |
| Gothic / alien ruin | Strange purpose, ancient scale, uncertainty with recoverable navigation | Traverse the occupied sliver while recognizing larger surrounding structures | Black fog hiding navigation defects; making all surfaces equally noisy or gigantic |
| Combat arena | Mechanic-specific movement, exposure, usable choices, camera room, fair threat information | Run a representative encounter using actual attacks/AI if available | Cover, balconies, chokepoints or symmetry added without a mechanic that uses them |
| Linear adventure | Authored sequence with local agency, anticipation, payoff and varied engagement | Replay the route while deliberately turning away from the staged focal point | Converting every corridor into a vista reveal or forcing the camera to hide weak staging |
| Puzzle / traversal space | Discoverable rules, readable state changes, fair testing and reset | Observe a new player learning the rule without explanation | Mistaking obscurity for difficulty or decorative routes for meaningful choices |
| Open exploration region | Local orientation, destinations, route identity, encounters with place, optional knowledge | Sample several approaches to a landmark and recovery after a detour | Increasing acreage before establishing local interest; landmarks everywhere |

## Functional worldbuilding at graybox fidelity

Ask what the place was for, who uses it, and what has changed. Reserve routes for arrival, service, defense and everyday activity only where relevant to the brief. A castle's service court and a monastery's cloister should not become the same room arrangement because both use stone walls.

Communicate history with spatial consequences: a collapsed span diverts circulation; a later settlement occupies an older oversized foundation; a barricade creates a defensive threshold. Use masses and relationships before detailed storytelling props. Do not imply that every empty space needs a lore explanation.

## Adapt encounter guidance to actual mechanics

Determine attack ranges, movement options, enemy approach behavior, camera limits and failure/recovery rules before shaping an encounter. Make a range/clearance sketch from those values. In a turn-based RPG, the exploration route and battle-stage needs may differ; do not assume real-time dodge corridors. In a non-combat game, replace encounter language with the actual task or movement challenge.

No functional enemy implementation means encounter geometry is a hypothesis, not balanced combat. Ask which configuration should be tested first, and preserve room for revision.

## Use the game's desired uncertainty

A mystery may deliberately withhold the destination while making local choices legible. A frequently visited hub should make routine tasks convenient. A maze may invite disorientation while preserving a way to recover. Explain which uncertainty serves the experience and which merely wastes attention.

Use [spatial patterns](spatial-patterns.md) as optional structures, and [awe](awe-and-perceived-scale.md) when grandeur is part of the brief. A quiet, intimate space can be the correct choice even in a monumental world.
