# Small revisions with explicit tests

These are original, illustrative design exercises, not reports of built or playtested levels. “Before” describes a hypothetical weak version; “change” is a proposal, not a measured improvement. Read the relevant [guidance](../../references/player-guidance.md), [storytelling](../../references/environmental-storytelling.md) or [awe](../../references/awe-and-perceived-scale.md) reference. Resolve actual dimensions through the project's controller, camera and footprint before construction.

## A — The aqueduct refuge

**Brief:** a compact, walking-only fantasy ruin with a required refuge, optional inspection gallery and safe overlook. Make ancient infrastructure feel vast. Keep the approved main route, entrances, footprint and movement rules. No mandatory jump, added climbing or moving machinery.

**Before:** disconnected ruin masses and scattered rubble imply no particular event. A huge backdrop has no familiar scale reference. Scenic shelf edges resemble the local walking route. The existing return path exposes the opening from above, but there is nothing distinctive to recognize.

Try these as separate patches, not one indivisible redesign:

| Patch | Concrete graybox change | What to observe, and what would contradict the proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Clear the false route | Break the scenic shelf's walkable-looking continuity beyond the existing boundary; leave the actual path and its access cues intact | Record attempts to enter the shelf and recovery. Continued attempts may indicate another misleading cue or an unrecognized boundary. |
| Suggest a shared event | Align the breached channel and displaced slab masses along one plausible failure direction; keep all route clearance reserved | Ask what might have happened and which shapes suggested it. Accept different histories; failure to notice any relationship means the arrangement is still unproven. |
| Connect small place to large system | Give the refuge an ordinary door-scale proxy and a distinctive support shape. From the existing overlook, expose how that same support belongs to the aqueduct continuing beyond the boundary | Ask what became newly understandable, then ask separately about feeling and scale. A recognized support with no emotional change is not evidence of awe. |

Use a few large masses and a channel void before small debris or surface detail. Let the gallery suggest maintenance or adaptation without requiring a lore explanation to find the refuge. A distant continuation stays scenic; the local task remains short. Retest the approach, optional route and return, including permitted off-axis camera views.

## B — The reopened conservatory

**Brief:** a peaceful public garden. Reach the central greenhouse and optionally visit a seed exchange. Keep the existing free camera, assistance settings and routes. No combat, locks or destructive backstory is needed.

**Before:** identical shed fronts make the greenhouse entrance indistinguishable. Every path receives the same bright marker. Decorative roof ledges repeat the shape used for reachable platforms elsewhere in the game.

**Change:** first test a more recognizable entrance mass from the actual decision point; preserve the optional path's distinct destination. In a separate patch, change only the unreachable roof edge's misleading profile. For history, align an old growing bed with a larger new greenhouse foundation, leaving an everyday service passage between them: expansion and care become a possible reading, without prop clutter.

**Test:** distinguish choosing the seed exchange from being unable to find the greenhouse. Log unsupported roof attempts separately. Ask what the arrangement suggests about use over time; no exact historical answer is required. Keep needed assistance active, and compare optional marker treatments only under documented conditions. Do not claim the greenhouse is easier to find from a top-down plan alone.
