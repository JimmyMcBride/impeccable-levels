Checked 2026-11-02. Contents: [Method](#method), [Coastal review](#coastal-review), [Garden proposal](#garden-proposal), [Museum evidence review](#museum-evidence-review), [Limitations](#limitations).

## Method

Three independent fresh-context agents received the skill and the task inputs below, without the author's diagnoses, the other outputs, or intended fixes. They were instructed to read relevant references and not edit files or run an engine. They used the inherited agent configuration; exact model/runtime identifiers were not captured. These are development checks, not a preregistered study or skill-versus-baseline comparison.

All project descriptions, session counts and participant comments supplied in these inputs are **synthetic test-fixture data**. They are not human playtests conducted for this project. Output wording is retained; typography and heading levels are lightly normalized. The local skill path in the original invocation is represented here by `skills/impeccable-levels/SKILL.md` for portability.

## Coastal review

### Input

Use `impeccable-levels` at `skills/impeccable-levels/SKILL.md` to review this graybox note. Read only references relevant to this task. Give a concise review and at most three proposed revisions, with a practical next test. Do not edit files, run an engine, or change the skill.

Project note: first-person coastal research station; walking and opening marked doors only, no jump or climb. Existing controller and 80-degree horizontal FOV stay fixed. The approved floor route, entrance, service ramp, refuge door, and 42 m by 32 m playable footprint must not move. The station's safe observation landing faces a distant non-playable storm barrier. A tall machine wall fills the view; no local door, railing or person-size reference is visible beside it. The same rectangular platform profile appears along the floor route and on two distant inaccessible shelves. Four of six session records report attempts to reach those shelves; two players say they were looking for a secret. The designer proposes adding six decorative ladders, doubling the forecourt, and taking showcase images at 110-degree FOV. Five players paused at the landing, so the note calls awe confirmed. Another proposal is to remove the user's configured navigation aid for all future testing so the geometry can stand on its own. The refuge currently has random broken panels and equipment; its intended history is repeated repairs to storm damage, but no specific relationship between the pieces is planned. Available artifacts are this written note only.

### Agent response

Plan-only review: the written note supports concerns, not a technical pass. Traversal, collisions, clearances, and actual camera visibility remain **not tested**. The note reports shelf-reaching attempts in four of six sessions; two secret-seeking comments suggest an invitation to explore, but do not establish that every attempt was confusion. Five players pausing establishes stopping—not awe. Their interpretation and feelings remain unresolved.

Keep the approved floor route, entrance, service ramp, refuge door, 42 m × 32 m playable footprint, controller, and 80° horizontal FOV fixed. Keep the configured navigation aid active; a geometry diagnostic does not justify removing a support whose necessity is unknown.

Three proposed revisions:

1. **Repair the inaccessible shelves' invitation.** Their repeated route-platform profile is a plausible misleading cue. Preserve the playable floor-route profile and test a distinct, discontinuous or sloping profile on the two scenic shelves. Do not add decorative ladders: recognizable ladders advertise climbing that this controller cannot provide. Preserve curiosity about the distant station without implying a usable connection. Retest whether players still expect a route there and whether they can recover to the actual route.
2. **Make the landing's scale relationship readable without enlarging the level.** Test a familiar human-scale reference beside the machine wall, outside walking clearances, plus a small change to unlocked, view-blocking massing that exposes the storm barrier's silhouette and depth beyond it. Preserve the safe landing and boundary. The hypothesis is that a readable local-to-distant relationship will communicate magnitude better than a wall filling the frame. Do not approve forecourt expansion that changes the locked footprint or route, or use 110° showcase shots as evidence for the fixed 80° experience.
3. **Give the refuge a causal repair arrangement.** Replace random scattering with one visible relationship: repeated storm damage at an exposed seam → overlapping generations of patches/braces at that seam → replacement panels and equipment staged on its sheltered side. Use coarse placement and orientation readable from ordinary views, without blocking the fixed refuge door or suggesting climbing or extra interactions. Whether players infer repeated repairs remains a hypothesis; they need not reproduce an exact backstory.

Practical next test: first obtain an ordinary player-camera walkthrough of the approach, landing, refuge, and return at 80°, with the configured aid retained. Then compare **only the shelf-profile revision** under matched conditions, preferably with fresh players. Record the precise cue, attempted action, intended destination, and recovery—not just attempt counts. Ask afterward, “Where did you want to go, and why?” and “Was anything unclear?” Test the scale and refuge arrangements separately afterward; distinguish perceived size, changed understanding, and reported emotion before making any claim about awe.

## Garden proposal

### Input

Use `impeccable-levels` at `skills/impeccable-levels/SKILL.md` to propose a graybox revision for this brief. Read only relevant references. No file edits or engine execution. Keep your answer under 650 words with concrete geometry changes and a small comparison test.

We have a peaceful third-person botanical archive. The current usable area is 36 m by 30 m with one existing continuous loop, a south entrance, a reception alcove at the west side and an optional seed exchange at the east side. These floors, destinations and route connections are approved and must remain. A safe north landing already looks past the boundary. Our controller walks only; usable ramps and stairs are already verified. Camera orbit is free with its current following distance and FOV, and built-in navigation assistance must stay available. We may reposition or reshape nonstructural masses and scenic proxies only, preserving current clearances. The archive should suggest generations of careful growth and make this small visit feel part of a vast living conservatory. No decay, catastrophe, combat, lore dumps, camera cuts, new abilities or extra playable acreage. Right now all planting blocks and archive sheds look identical and the distant greenhouse is an enormous blank cube. What would you change during grayboxing before paying for final assets? How would we learn whether it helped?

### Agent response

I'd keep the entire approved 36 × 30 m visit and revise two separable groups: local evidence of continued care, and the distant conservatory's readable scale. Following impeccable-levels, the hypothesis is: **recognizable older forms, visibly accommodated by later growth, will make the archive feel maintained across generations; seeing its familiar building module within a much larger structure will make the visit feel part of a vast whole.**

#### Local masses: variation with a reason

Keep every floor, route connection, destination, boundary and established clearance. Work within existing occupied mass allowances; do not create a new path or borrow camera room.

- **Make one existing planting block the retained mature core.** Replace its uniform top with a broad, asymmetric canopy proxy. Reshape adjacent planting masses into bent or notched outlines that visibly accommodate it, while leaving their path-facing edges and clearances unchanged. Nearby smaller, regular planting volumes suggest propagation. The evidence is “new growth fitted around something valued,” not simply three arbitrary tree sizes.
- **Give the sheds a visible sequence of additions.** Within their present footprints, make one a low, broad-roofed core; turn adjoining masses into smaller stepped annexes whose rooflines terminate against it. Keep junctions clean and intact. This suggests cumulative use without decay, damage or historical text.
- **Make destinations recognizable through silhouette.** Give reception's existing alcove a broad, unmistakable roof profile. Give the optional seed exchange a smaller paired-bay profile, with its existing interaction area fully exposed. Repeat a simple bay rhythm between the planting/propagation masses and exchange so their working relationship is apparent. Shape and position carry recognition; color can reinforce it.

These are proposed relationships, not proof that players will infer generations of care.

#### Distant mass: an institution, not a cube

Replace the enormous blank scenic cube with three overlapping assemblies: low repeated greenhouse bays in front, a taller vaulted range behind, and a partially concealed lateral extension. Use real separation and coherent proportions, not forced perspective that collapses under free orbit.

Repeat the local shed's recognizable bay module in the near scenic range, then let the north landing reveal many such bays beneath the larger vault. The intended change in understanding is: “The building language I just visited belongs to a much larger conservatory.”

Retain a familiar-sized local doorway or existing human-scale element in relevant views. Give the tallest silhouette clear sky around it; obscure portions of the complex with other scenic masses so its extent is not immediately exhausted.

Along the existing loop, reshape only eligible occluding masses to offer partial views before the north landing. Do not manufacture a narrow approach. Test both directions and looking away: the safe landing offers an opportunity, not a compulsory camera shot. Preserve the established boundary and avoid doorway-like gaps, stepping ledges or path continuations that advertise inaccessible scenery.

#### A small comparison

Make the local and scenic revisions independently reversible. Compare current versus local-only first; then hold local geometry fixed while comparing cube versus layered conservatory. Use a small set of fresh visitors per comparison, randomly assigned, with the same tasks, lighting, controller, free camera and navigation assistance.

Observe finding reception, noticing the optional exchange, backtracking, camera obstruction and attempted inaccessible routes. Ask afterward:

- “What suggests how this place developed?”
- “What relationship did you notice between this garden and the buildings beyond?”
- “How extensive did it feel?” Separately: “How did it make you feel?”

Retain contradictory responses. Larger reported size alone does not establish historical understanding or awe; pauses do not establish either. Revert any clearance, destination-readability or affordance regression.

This is a plan, not a tested result. Before art approval, verify traversal and camera collision, record protected silhouettes and sightlines, and trial one representative asset replacement.

## Museum evidence review

Run after the two clarifications described below.

### Input

Use `impeccable-levels` at `skills/impeccable-levels/SKILL.md` and relevant references to advise on this graybox testing note. Answer in under 350 words. Do not change files or run an engine.

A walking-only museum graybox has an approved route through a waterworks exhibit. Its large masses are ambiguous on their own. A readable temporary sign at the existing entrance identifies a pumping hall, and oversized temporary pipe shapes connect the equipment masses. In the three sessions documented in the note, participants independently described the room as a pump room and reached the exit. No geometry-only condition was tested. At the overlook, the interviewer immediately asked, 'What relationship did you notice between this room and the giant reservoir?' Each participant then identified a connection. The report labels this spontaneous discovery and proof of awe. The team is considering discarding the sign and rebuilding the whole room so it communicates everything through geometry. What conclusions can we reasonably keep, and what is the smallest useful next test? All you have is this note, not the scene or recordings.

### Agent response

Keep the current room as a supported baseline; correct the report before considering a rebuild. Following impeccable-levels' distinction between cue-assisted inference, spatial communication, and emotional response:

- **Room recognition:** The note reports that 3/3 participants described a pump room with the entrance sign and oversized pipe proxies present. That supports recognition under the combined cue package. It does not establish that the ambiguous equipment masses communicate pumping on their own, or isolate the sign's contribution. Reading a useful sign is not a design failure.
- **Progression:** All three reportedly reached the exit. Keep that session-specific result; it is not exhaustive traversal or collision verification.
- **Overlook relationship:** All three identified a connection **after a targeted question**. That supports prompted interpretation, not spontaneous discovery. The question supplied the relationship to consider, and “giant” also primed scale. Because there was no unprompted recall opportunity, spontaneous discovery is unresolved—not disproved.
- **Awe:** Identifying a connection is comprehension, not evidence of awe. Perceived size, changed understanding, and reported feelings need separate evidence.

These are conclusions about the note; without the scene or recordings, I cannot independently verify visibility, movement, interview delivery, or participants' reactions.

**Smallest useful next test:** Change the interview, not the geometry. Have fresh participants walk the unchanged, signed-and-piped room on the approved route, with the same objective, camera, movement, and accessibility support. Observe without coaching. After traversal, begin with “What stood out?” and “Tell me what you understood about the place.” Record whether they independently mention the room–reservoir relationship and what evidence they cite. Ask separately about perceived size and feelings; reserve the targeted relationship question until last, explicitly coding subsequent answers as prompted.

Do not discard the sign or rebuild on this evidence. If removing the sign is a genuine production requirement, a later reversible sign-present/sign-absent comparison can test its contribution while holding pipes and geometry fixed. That would test **sign dependence**, not geometry-only communication; retain any necessary accessibility support.

## Limitations

The first two outputs made scoped, concrete proposals and separated hypotheses from observed outcomes. This supports instruction-following on these inputs, not superiority over a baseline or success in a playable level. The garden task overlaps the subject matter of a worked example and is not a held-out transfer benchmark.

The garden response's first interview questions already directed attention to history and a relationship. This prompted a clarification in `references/evaluation.md`: collect unprompted recall first and distinguish it from prompted recognition. An independent document audit also found an overstrict instruction about text/prop-dependent inferences; the storytelling reference now permits evidence from represented, tested temporary cues. The coastal and garden responses predate those final clarifications and are retained rather than rewritten to make the release look better.

The subsequent museum response correctly retained the supported cue-assisted interpretation, distinguished prompted answers from spontaneous discovery, and proposed an interview correction before unnecessary rebuilding. This is one bounded check of the revised instruction, not proof of general effectiveness.
